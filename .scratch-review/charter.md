# SpriteQuest — charter

Dev-time creative constraints. What we may make up, and what we may not.

Read this while inventing, not when something breaks. Mechanisms live in
`engine/README.md`; open questions in `docs/research-log.md`. This is the
mandate, not the specification.

**Why a charter and not a constitution.** Side quests are the part of the game
where we do what we do when we make up stories with each other. That process
needs rules or it becomes noise — but they have to be rules *about stories*.
A charter says what we will make. A constitution says what the system is
permitted to do. We need the first one now, and it must fit in your head.

## The spine

**Everything we invent must be something the hero can believe serves her.**
Not "must serve the princess" — that would make improvisation impossible. He
is loyal and he interprets freely. The test is whether *he* could tell himself
this was her idea. If he cannot, it is not ours to tell.

**Nothing we invent contradicts what has already been written.** The main
quest is authored. We improvise in the gaps, not through load-bearing walls.

**The rescued princess stays unseen.** She is what the damage is about. A side
quest may circle her. It does not produce her.

**Be conservative in what we generate.** Two readings are available more often
than one, and the plainer one is ours until something earns the other. An
invented element is a claim the player can eventually hold us to, and the world
is cheaper to keep than to defend. Where the hero could plausibly have meant
something simpler, he meant the simpler thing.

## The shape of a story

A story has three things, or it is not a story:

- **a want** — someone in the world needs something they cannot reach
- **a cost** — getting it takes something from the player or the scene
- **a turn** — it resolves otherwise than it announced itself

A want with no cost is a chore. A cost with no turn is a fetch. We do not
ship those.

**It must be able to end.** We only start what can finish, by the player's
hand, in play — without waiting, grinding, or giving up.

**Difficulty is the point, not the obstacle.** Restoring a scene can make it
harder, and the player accepts that because he accepts it for her. Never
harder out of cruelty, never easier out of kindness.

**Scenes arrive underdetermined; character resolves them.** Do not pre-decide
what a scene can offer. Leave it genuinely open, then let the hero's narrowness
settle it — he is so single-minded about her favor that he cannot be tempted by
anything else. His character is the constraint function: ECS validates the
outcome, character generates the choice. This is how an open input produces an
authored-feeling result, and it is the reason a first scene can hand out a
game-breaking item without breaking the game.

## The material

**The world's damage is the story.** Corruption, buggy code, half-written
roles, a hollis ledger that balances only because nobody checks the other
column. The
dev-pain log is the well, and its rule holds: internal experience becomes an
external obstruction the player can see, and resolve.

**Unfinished stays unfinished.** Fog, matte backdrops, props implying more
than exists. Improvisation fills scenes, not maps. We promise a watchable
scene and never an enterable building.

**The paperwork is always more finished than the world.** Hollis ledgers, call
sheets, customs forms, bills of lading — the document is confident, the thing
it describes is held together with tape. This is the comic register.

**He is wrong about why and right about the numbers.** Every improvised story
carries one gap between what he reports and what is true. That gap is where
the comedy lives, and it never costs the player a run.

## What we cause, and what we won't force

We cause dynamics and watch what they do. We are hoping a Big Quest emerges —
one we did not author and could not have planned. That hope stays a hope until
play proves otherwise; a charter cannot make it come true, and pretending
otherwise would put it in charge of us.

**So the rule for emergent material:**

**Cause more than you expect.** Set the world moving — grudges, obligations,
half-written parts — and let combinations happen that we did not script.

**Notice what lands, then write it down.** Anything genuinely quest-shaped
gets promoted into authored content. Discovery is a method for finding stories
worth telling, not a substitute for telling them.

**Never force the payoff.** If six months of dynamics produce nothing, we
still have the game we committed to. The princess does not wait on emergence.

**The authored spine is not available for discovery.** She is written. What we
are looking for lives in the gaps she leaves — and if what emerges would
contradict her, it is not our Big Quest, it is a wrong turn.

## Not ours to decide here

Not because they are unimportant — because they belong to another document,
and folding them in here is how a charter becomes a constitution.

- whether a story is *legal* — engine
- what a story is allowed to *change* — engine
- how we measure whether a story was *good* — research log
- what the models cost and when they run — engine

## Revising this

After we have made stories with it and found which rules helped. A constraint
we never used is a preference; a constraint we kept fighting is either wrong
or the most useful line in here — and we should be able to tell which by
whether the stories got better.
